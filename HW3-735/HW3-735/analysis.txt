Bigger matrices give better speedup, going from about 4.8x at k=10 up to the best result of 28.98x at k=14 with leaf size 512. The efficiency goes up the same way, from about 0.10 to 0.604. This is because big matrices have much more work to share among the 48 threads, so the overhead of starting tasks matters less. So the best result in the whole test is k=14 (n=16384) with a leaf of 512.

For a given matrix size, the speedup does not just keep rising with leaf size; it goes up, peaks, then goes down. If the leaf is too small (around 2^4), there are too many tiny tasks and the scheduling overhead takes over, but if it is too big (around 2^10), there is not enough work left to parallelize. The best leaf gets bigger as the matrix gets bigger, ending around 2^7 to 2^9 (128 to 512) for the largest matrices, though the k=10 and k=11 rows are noisy because those runs are very short. Overall, a leaf size between 128 and 512 gives the most speedup for large matrices, with the peak at k=14 and leaf 512 (28.98x speedup and 0.604 efficiency).
